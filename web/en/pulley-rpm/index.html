Pulley RPM formula

In a belt drive without slip, the belt's linear speed is the same on both pulleys. That gives:

N1 × D1 = N2 × D2

Where N1 is the driver pulley RPM, D1 its diameter, N2 the driven pulley RPM and D2 its diameter. Solving for each term:

  • N2 = N1 × D1 / D2
  • N1 = N2 × D2 / D1
  • D2 = N1 × D1 / N2
  • D1 = N2 × D2 / N1

Worked example

A 1750 RPM motor with a 100 mm pulley driving a 200 mm pulley:

N2 = 1750 × 100 / 200 = 875 RPM

The larger pulley turns slower: speed is halved and available torque rises by the same factor (ignoring losses).

How to choose pulley diameters

To reduce speed, use a driven pulley larger than the driver; to increase it, do the opposite. Respect the minimum pulley diameter recommended by the belt maker, since very small pulleys shorten belt life. In practice, belt slip puts the real RPM 1% to 3% below the theoretical value on V-belts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate the driven pulley RPM?

Multiply the motor RPM by the driver pulley diameter and divide by the driven pulley diameter: N2 = N1 × D1 / D2.

Does a bigger pulley turn faster or slower?

Slower. The larger the driven pulley relative to the driver, the lower the output RPM and the higher the torque.

Can I use inches instead of millimetres?

Yes, as long as both pulleys use the same unit. The ratio depends only on the proportion between the diameters.
