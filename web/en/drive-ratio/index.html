Pulley drive ratio calculator

The drive ratio tells you how many turns the driver pulley makes for each turn of the driven one. Enter both diameters to see the ratio and the type of drive.

Drive ratio formula

i = D2 / D1 = N1 / N2

When i > 1 the driven pulley is larger: speed is reduced and torque increases. When i < 1 speed increases and torque drops. With i = 1 both pulleys turn at the same speed.

Worked example

Driver pulley of 100 mm and driven pulley of 200 mm:

i = 200 / 100 = 2, that is, 1 : 2

With a 1750 RPM motor, the output is 1750 / 2 = 875 RPM, and output torque is roughly twice the motor torque, minus losses.

Multi-stage drives

When the required reduction is large (above 6:1 or 8:1 for belts), split it into two stages. The overall ratio is the product of each stage's ratio: total i = i1 × i2. This keeps pulleys a reasonable size and preserves the wrap angle.

Frequently asked questions

What does a 1:2 drive ratio mean?

The driver pulley makes two turns for every turn of the driven one: speed is halved and torque doubles, ignoring losses.

What is the difference between reduction and speed increase?

In a reduction the driven pulley is larger (i > 1) and turns slower. In a speed increase it is smaller (i < 1) and turns faster.

How do I calculate the drive ratio from RPM?

Divide the driver pulley RPM by the driven pulley RPM: i = N1 / N2.
